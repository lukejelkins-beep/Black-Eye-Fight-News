# Images

## Where we are (measured 2026-11-17)
- `data/results.json` holds 884 fighter photo references. **None are hosted by BEFN.** 570 point at ESPN's CDN, 235 at PFL's S3 buckets, 70 at ONE's CDN. News thumbnails come straight from publishers (MMA Fighting, Sherdog, World Boxing News, Wikimedia).
- The repository holds about nine images. The Sumo preview is the exception: 190 official JSA portraits in `assets/sumo-photos/`, with a rights record per photo in `data/sumo/approved-photos.json`.
- Consequences: the look is uneven (every source crops differently), images can disappear or be blocked at any time, we cannot resize or optimise them, and rights depend on someone else's server.

## Target
Every image shown to readers is a file BEFN controls, in a consistent shape, with a recorded source and rights status.

## Pipeline (proposed)
1. **Intake:** original goes in a private store (not the public site root) with source URL, credit, licence/permission and date.
2. **Process:** generate fixed crops per use — portrait 4:5, card 16:9, thumbnail 1:1 — in WebP/AVIF at 1x and 2x. Face-aware crop so heads are never cut.
3. **Publish:** only the processed files go in a public folder, e.g. `assets/img/<sport>/<id>-<crop>.webp`.
4. **Record:** one register entry per image: id, source, credit, licence, approved-by, date.
5. **Use:** pages reference the register, never a raw third-party URL.

## Rules
- Hot-linking third-party images is a stop-gap, not the end state. Migrate by priority: Top 25 UFC and boxing names first, then event headliners, then the rest.
- No image without a rights status. "Found online" is not a status.
- Event posters: use the promotion's own poster when permitted; otherwise a BEFN-designed card from the headline bout.
- Alt text on every image; names and event, not "photo".

## Local library (decided 2026-11-17)
There is no existing stockpile. The owner keeps a local `befn-images/` folder on their Mac with `originals/`, `register.csv` (source, credit, licence, permission, focus point, approval) and `process_images.py`, which makes the portrait, card and thumbnail WebP crops and a `manifest.json`. Only approved rows are processed. Processed files and the manifest are what get added to the site.
- Claude does not fetch photos of public figures from the web. The owner downloads what they are permitted to use and drops it into `originals/`.
- **Exception, Sumo (owner approved 2026-11-19):** official JSA rikishi portraits are fetched automatically by `scripts/sumo-portrait-intake.py` on the Sumo preview branch, under the owner's attested JSA permission (2026-11-16). Each one is matched to the roster by JSA id, ring name and stable, recorded in `data/sumo/approved-photos.json` with source, credit, licence and hash, and turned into a display cut-out when it is a clean full-body photo. Runs daily from `.github/workflows/sumo-portrait-intake.yml`. No other sport or source is covered by this exception.

## Boxing cutouts (added 2026-11-22)
The boxers BEFN holds photographs of get the same treatment as the UFC fighters: a transparent cutout made offline from the photograph already in `assets/img/boxing/` by `scripts/build-boxing-cutouts.py` (rembg `isnet-general-use`, no web fetch, no new photograph). The background removal, the keep/drop checks and the head-and-shoulders crop are imported from `scripts/build-ufc-cutouts.py` rather than copied, so the two sports cannot drift apart. Results and reasons are in `data/boxing-cutout-register.json`, and every kept row repeats the credit and rights of the photograph it came from, so a cutout can never be shown with less provenance than its source. A boxer who fails keeps the photograph in a disc.
- First run: **33 kept of 42**. Three were dropped by the automatic frame check and six by eye.
- The automatic check (`judge_frame`) catches the crop that goes wrong in one specific way: `frame()` finds the head from the mask alone, and on a full-length photograph with no clear neck it treats the whole figure as the head, so the cutout comes out as a whole boxer at thumbnail size. Such a crop leaves the frame mostly empty either side, so a figure spanning under 65% of the frame width means the head was not found. It dropped Adam Azim (43%), Conah Walker (49%) and Hassan Azim (61%).
- It is a backstop, not a substitute for looking. Six more were dropped by eye and are named in `BY_EYE` in the script, with the reason, so a later run does not take them back: Alberto Palmetta (a microphone across the chin), Ishmael Davis (a forearm over the face), Lauren Price (the belts fill the frame), Ryan Garcia (a weigh-in face-off with two masked people in it), Teremoana Teremoana (mid-punch, the torso fills the frame) and William Crolla (a full-length flexing pose). Press kits carry action, weigh-in and press-conference shots as often as portraits, and no mask check can see that a face is turned away or behind a glove, so **every batch wants a look by eye**.
- Second run (2026-11-24): 10 of the 13 new photographs kept a cut-out, **43 kept of 55** in all. Josh Kelly's press-kit source is under 300 px wide, and two were dropped by eye: Naoya Inoue (the trophy in front of him leaves shards and holes in the jacket) and Teófimo López (a dark object behind his head is kept as part of the figure).
- The pages read cut-outs through one register per sport: `assets/app-inline-2.js` fetches both and `BEFNLanding.mergeCutouts` tags each row with the folder its file sits in, so a boxer's cutout is read from `assets/img/boxing-cutouts/` and a UFC fighter's from `assets/img/ufc-cutouts/`. A slug held in both sports is dropped, the same rule the local image map uses. A register written before boxing cutouts existed has no folder on its rows and still resolves to the UFC one.
- A cut-out is a picture BEFN holds, so it is drawn borderless and seamless like the UFC ones. The press-kit photograph it came from is not: a publisher's crop stays its own.

## Boxing: Wikimedia Commons as the second source (added 2026-11-22)
Press kits reach a promoter's own stable, not the boxers the headlines talk about, so the owner approved Wikimedia Commons as a second source. `scripts/boxing-portrait-intake.py` falls back to it for a boxer no allowlisted press kit carries.
- A file is taken only when it is hosted on Commons (not on en.wikipedia, where the non-free files live) under a free licence — CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public domain — and only when the English Wikipedia article calls the subject a boxer, which is what keeps another person of the same name out. Minimum 400 px wide.
- Every row records the photographer, the licence, the licence URL, the Commons file page and what was changed. 14 boxers came in this way, under CC BY-SA 4.0, CC BY 3.0, CC BY-SA 2.0, CC BY-SA 3.0, CC BY 4.0 and CC0.
- **Attribution.** CC BY and CC BY-SA ask for the photographer, licence and changes to be named where the work is used, which a photograph in a bout disc cannot do. `scripts/build-photo-credits.py` generates `boxing-photo-credits.html` from the register — Commons photographs with photographer and licence, press-kit photographs under the owner's attestation — and `index.html` links it in the footer, the same arrangement the Sumo credits page already uses. Run it after every intake, or the regression test fails.
- **A lead photograph is not always a portrait.** Four were fetched, looked at and dropped: a two-fighter exchange where the subject cannot be told apart, a ringside moment with the face turned away, a mid-fight frame too small at thumbnail size, and a training shot with a hand across the face. They are listed in `REVIEWED_OUT` in the script so a later run does not take them back. Any new Commons batch wants the same look.

## Photo runner (added 2026-11-24)
Owner request of 24 Nov 2026: "Can you create a terminal scraper/runner to get all the photos we dont have". `scripts/photo-runner.py` finds every fighter on the site without an approved photo and runs each sport's existing pipeline for them in a **staging** mode; nothing goes live until a person has looked at the contact sheet and run `approve`.

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `python3 scripts/photo-runner.py missing [--sport boxing\|ufc\|mma\|sumo\|all]` | Who has no approved photo, and who has a photo but no cut-out, from the data the site uses: boxing champions and scouting roster in `COMBAT_DATA` (`assets/app-inline-1.js`), upcoming cards, results; UFC champions, cards, results, the Fighters hub (`data/ufc-fighter-form.json`) and the UFC register's `missing`; PFL, ONE (MMA), KSW, RIZIN and Cage Warriors fighters on cards and results; sumo `rikishi-index.json` against `approved-photos.json` and `cutouts.json`. Sorted by prominence (current champion, upcoming main event, upcoming card, recent result within 120 days, roster). Writes `qa/photo-runner/missing.json` and `missing.md` with why each matters and the last attempt (no source, blocked host, rejected by eye, name mismatch). |
| `python3 scripts/photo-runner.py fetch [--sport …] [--limit N] [--names "A,B"] [--retry]` | Runs the pipelines in staging mode for the missing people (skipping anyone tried in the last 6 days unless `--retry`): crops into `qa/photo-runner/staging/<run-id>/`, a `manifest.json` with every register field (source page, image URL, credit, licence or attestation, SHA-256, size, fetch date) and `contact.png`, the name under each photograph. Registers nothing and writes nothing under `assets/` or `data/`. |
| `python3 scripts/photo-runner.py approve <run-id> (--all \| --only "A,B") [--reject "C:reason,D:reason"] [--owner-cleared] [--no-cutouts]` | Registers the approved ones as each pipeline does (register rows, files in `assets/img/…`), records rejects with the reason in the pipeline's own reviewed-out list so they are not fetched again, builds their cut-outs, writes `cutouts.png` (a contact sheet of the new cut-outs), regenerates `assets/local-images.js`, `boxing-photo-credits.html` and `mma-photo-credits.html`, and keeps both sheets and a `summary.md` in `qa/photo-runner/reviews/<run-id>/`. UFC ESPN headshots need `--owner-cleared`, which writes the owner's statement into the UFC register's rights note, as `pull-ufc-hires-photos.py apply` always has. |
| `python3 scripts/photo-runner.py reject-cutout "<name>:reason"` | Drops a cut-out by eye: adds it to `BY_EYE` (`build-boxing-cutouts.py`, `BY_EYE_MMA`, or `build-ufc-cutouts.py`), marks the register row and deletes the file. |
| `python3 scripts/photo-runner.py drop <folder> --sport … --credit "…" --attest "owner, YYYY-MM-DD: <wording>"` | Stages the owner's own photographs; the file name is the fighter's name (`Agit Kabayel.jpg`). A file whose name matches no fighter on the site for that sport, or a fighter who already has a photo, is refused. Same contact sheet and `approve`; the row records `rights: owner-supplied`, the credit, the original file name and the attestation text, and the credits page lists it. |
| `python3 scripts/photo-runner.py drop <folder> --kind fullbody --sport ufc\|boxing --credit "…" --attest "owner, YYYY-MM-DD: <wording>"` | Full-length photographs the owner supplies for the champions carousel on the Analysis pages (added 2026-11-25, owner's choice "Drop-folder full bodies"). Same naming rule (`Ciryl Gane.jpg`), same staging, `contact.png` and `approve`; a fighter is refused only when a full-body cut-out is already kept for them, not for having a portrait. `approve` cuts the whole figure out with rembg (`fullbody_cutout` in `scripts/build-ufc-cutouts.py`: one connected figure, 8-80% of the frame, at least 60% of its height, source at least 800 px tall), trims it to the figure, writes `assets/img/fullbody-cutouts/<slug>.webp` and a row in `data/fullbody-cutout-register.json` (`ok`, `sport`, `name`, `source` = file name and SHA-256, `credit`, `attestation`). A figure that fails the checks is recorded `ok: false` with the reason and no file. The original photograph is not published. `--no-cutouts` is refused for these, since the cut-out is the only thing registered. The carousel shows a full-body cut-out only when `ok` is true, the sport matches and the attestation is present; otherwise it falls back to the head-and-shoulders cut-out, then the portrait, then initials. |
| `python3 scripts/photo-runner.py report` | Missing per sport, what the last run staged and added, and the hosts that turned it away. |

How each sport is fetched (the runner chooses who; the pipelines fetch, unchanged in their rules):
- **Boxing:** `boxing-portrait-intake.py --names-file F --stage DIR` (press kits under the attestations of 2026-11-22 and 2026-11-24, then Commons).
- **UFC:** `pull-ufc-hires-photos.py --work DIR fetch --new-fighters F` (ESPN headshots, the source of the owner-cleared UFC library), then `process-fighter-images.py` for the crops; `approve` hands the approved ones to that script's own `apply --owner-cleared`. The runner reads `robots.txt` first: `site.web.api.espn.com/robots.txt` answers 403, so it passes `--no-search` and only ESPN ids already in BEFN's data are used. `process-fighter-images.py` now lays a transparent PNG on white: the colour under ESPN's transparent pixels showed as streaks (the 137 ESPN headshots added on 2026-11-24 carry them and want re-processing from their originals).
- **MMA (other promotions):** `mma-portrait-intake.py --stage DIR` (above).
- **Sumo:** `sumo-portrait-intake.py --update-batch 0 --stage DIR` (official JSA portraits, identity by JSA id, ring name and stable); `approve` records them with the intake's own `record_photo`, which also makes the cut-out.

The weekly GitHub job (`.github/workflows/photo-runner.yml`, Thursdays 02:43 UTC, and on demand) runs `missing` and `fetch` for every sport, and when anything is staged runs `approve --all` **on a new branch** `auto/photo-runner-<run-id>` and opens a draft PR whose body shows both contact sheets and lists every photograph with its source and credit. It never pushes to `main`; merging the PR is the approval. Staging output is git-ignored (`qa/photo-runner/staging/`); the review sheets, `state.json` and the missing list are committed.
