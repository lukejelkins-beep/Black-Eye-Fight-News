Do UFC gyms decide fights?

We checked 544 UFC fights from 2025. Once you allow for who each fighter was going in, gyms look much the same.

Black Eye Fight News analysis, 28 Nov 2026

The short answer
  • Some gyms had far better years than others: City Kickboxing went 14-4, American Top Team 27-32.
  • But gyms with strong fighters win more because of the fighters. Allowing for each fighter's record going in, the gaps shrink to what luck produces.
  • Shuffling fighters between gyms at random gave gym-to-gym differences as large as the real ones in 60% of 2,000 tries.

What stood out

City Kickboxing won 14 of 18, about 5 wins more than its fighters' records suggested. It is the best result on the chart, but with 15 gyms compared, one of them looking this good by luck alone is not unusual. It would happen about one year in five.

American Top Team, the busiest gym, went 27-32, about 3 wins under expectation. That is also well within chance. Xtreme Couture (21-13) and MMA Lab (8-14) sit at either end for the same reason: a handful of fights either way.

Why gyms still matter to fans

This does not say training camps are pointless. It says that over one season, a gym's win-loss record mostly reflects the fighters it already has. A great gym that signs great fighters will win a lot, and the record alone cannot tell you which came first.

How we did it

We took every decided UFC bout in 2025 (544 fights across 48 events, including Road to UFC). For each fighter we worked out their record going into the fight and, from the gap between the two, how likely each was to win. That method picked the winner in 58.5% of bouts. A gym's expected wins are the sum of those chances.

Limits: gyms are where Sherdog lists a fighter training now, not necessarily at the time of the fight, and a fighter who lists several gyms counts for each. Expectations come from records, not betting odds. One season is enough to rule out big gym effects, not small ones.
